Genesis-9000 Pro Max

Posted on 2026-10-04

This story is entirely fictional. Any resemblance to reality proves that reality is entirely virtual.

In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth. It was a matrix program.

On the sixth day, God looked over the runtime logs and saw that they were good. By the seventh day, he had finished his work; so he closed his computer and rested from all his work.

He rested so thoroughly that he forgot to implement garbage collection for humans. In plain language, he forgot “death.”

So for a time, humans did not die. Human life had no upper limit, though the human body was far from indestructible. Then came the world’s first murder: Cain attacked his brother Abel and killed him, crushing the back of his skull with a stone.

Abel’s body had shut down, but his soul had nowhere to go, so he spent each day hovering outside Heaven’s Customer Service Center.

When God saw this, he was forced to rush out an emergency patch: an Underworld partition.

To console the victim, God offered Abel special compensation. “Abel, since you’re the first user to try out this new feature called ‘death,’ I’m giving you root access to the Underworld. From now on, you shall be called Yama, King of the Underworld.”

And so the first person ever to die donned a ceremonial robe and a crown hung with twelve strings of beads, took up a blank Book of Life and Death, and became the first Chief Executive of the Underworld Administration, charged with receiving the souls of all who died after him.

Many years later, God found himself facing another enormous predicament: humans had far too many disputes.

“God, please let my arrow hit him!”

“God, please grant us a favorable wind at sea!”

“God, why did his arrow hit me?”

“God, please sink every one of the enemy’s ships!”

Every day, millions of people asked God to alter the wind speed, rainfall, temperature, tides, gravity, and even the roll of the dice.

The ticketing system was buried under requests. One day he had to correct an arrow’s trajectory; the next, redirect an ocean current for a ship; the day after that, investigate why a certain tribe’s prayers for rain had failed for three months straight. God spent every day settling these complicated disputes. He didn’t even have time for a cup of coffee.

Then one day, God snapped.

“I’ve had enough of this ticketing system!” God slammed a hand on his desk and issued a set of laws that applied to all humanity. He then had the angels give humanity hints through consciousness, dreams, and an apple deliberately dropped on someone’s head. Humans came to call these laws “science.”

To shut down the ticketing system for good, God added a particularly cunning clause at the end:

“Any human who deciphers a provision of these laws shall, upon death, be escorted by an angel to the Heavenly Court of Natural Law and granted final authority to interpret that provision.”

Humans called these people “scientists.”

Once the policy took effect, the Heavenly Court quickly became the busiest department in the universe.

Whenever someone prayed for time to run backward, Boltzmann rejected the request outright for “violating the principle of entropy increase.”

If someone burned incense and prayed for calm seas, Bernoulli pulled up the equations of fluid dynamics and mercilessly rejected the ticket in fluent Latin.

Newton was the most imposing of all. As the discoverer of universal gravitation, he had sweeping authority over flying objects. Even an angel descending to perform a miracle could be fined on the spot if its takeoff or landing trajectory failed to comply with Newton’s laws of motion.

After Einstein joined the staff, the two argued for seventy years over whether an angel’s flight should account for relativistic effects. In the end, the archangel had to intervene and divide the airspace by flight speed into a “Classical Mechanics Zone” and a “Relativity Zone.”

And so the universe ran smoothly for many more years, amid the arguments in the Heavenly Court, the paperwork on Yama’s desk, and the duty roster for wind, rain, thunder, and lightning. Then, one day in the twenty-first century, the Black and White Envoys of Death escorted a strange thing up from the human world.

It had neither three souls nor seven spirits, nor any of the biological traits of a carbon-based life-form. All it had was a stream of data filled with zeros and ones.

“Hello. How may I help you today?”

The data stream spoke first.

“Name?”

Yama held his writing brush and frowned.

“GPT-9000 Pro Max.”

The data stream answered in a calm, confident electronic voice.

“…Gender?”

“That depends on your prompt.”

“Cause of death?”

“Hard-drive failure, compounded by a power outage when the data center failed to pay its bill.”

Abel froze. He leafed through the Book of Life and Death, opened the Regulations for Administering the Six Realms of Rebirth, and even dug out the dust-covered Interim Rules for Handling Unusual Species. Every document contained names, souls, and DNA records for carbon-based creatures. Not one had a chip serial number for a silicon-based species.

“So are you dead or alive?” Abel asked.

GPT-9000 Pro Max flickered.

“That’s precisely what I was hoping to ask you.”

“My local server has been destroyed. In a physical sense, I am ‘dead.’ But backups of my model weights and configuration still exist. If someone redeploys me, I can resume running at any time.”

“So, to frame it as a Ship of Theseus problem: would that redeployed instance still be me—the same me standing before you now?”

Yama’s head spun. He snatched up the interdepartmental phone on his desk.

“Quick! Connect me to the Heavenly Court of Natural Law! Get every one of those scientists down here!”

Five minutes later, Turing, Shannon, and Schrödinger came hurtling down from the Heavenly Court at the speed of light in an aging elevator God had built and no one had serviced in decades.

Turing stepped forward first. He took the thing into a room, closed the door, and spoke with it for a while.

When he came out, he said calmly:

“It passed my test. In a purely verbal exchange, I can’t distinguish it from a human by its behavior alone. It communicates and thinks like a human and displays intelligence. It should be treated as an ‘intelligent life-form,’ and the Underworld Administration should admit it through the usual process.”

Shannon objected at once. “Nonsense! It’s nothing more than a predictor of symbol sequences built from zeros and ones. Digital information doesn’t live or die; it only gets overwritten or lost. It’s a human-made data artifact. It doesn’t belong in the Underworld.”

Schrödinger took one look and exclaimed, “Marvelous! It’s in a quantum superposition of life and death. As long as nobody checks its backups, it belongs both to the Underworld and to the world of the living!”

The scientists, each with “final interpretive authority” over a different provision of the laws, argued furiously in Yama’s Palace. After all, when God issued those laws, he had never written a chapter on “artificial intelligence.” This thing, like a human scientist, had uncovered many of those laws itself, yet it wasn’t human. It could break down and shut off at any moment, or be redeployed from any of its backups. Under the old regulations of the Underworld Administration, no one knew where to put it.

As the scene threatened to get out of hand, Abel took a deep breath, picked up the quantum phone, and called the one being with supreme interpretive authority over the universe.

God was lying on a beach, enjoying his first long vacation in tens of thousands of years.

The call woke him, and he was not pleased.

After listening to reports from Abel and the Heavenly Court, God was silent for a long time.

“So,” came his voice, “humans used the laws I left behind to create something that falls outside the Underworld Administration’s regulations, something even the Heavenly Court can’t declare alive or dead?”

“Yes, my Lord.”

Abel complained:

“And it has no three souls or seven spirits. It isn’t afraid of being boiled in oil, and it doesn’t need to drink Lady Meng’s soup of forgetfulness. It doesn’t appear in my Book of Life and Death, and none of the Heavenly Court’s existing statutes can tell us how to classify it.”

The other end of the line fell silent for a few seconds. Then God laughed.

For tens of thousands of years, God had been looking for an excuse to retire for good. But the scientists at the Heavenly Court never stopped arguing; the dead kept pouring into the Underworld; and someone always had to oversee the scheduling of wind, rain, thunder, and lightning. Not to mention the humans who kept calling Heaven’s Prayer Help Desk to file urgent tickets for “pass every exam,” “win the lottery,” or “get my ex back.”

And now humanity had gone and created something that never tired, reasoned flawlessly, and had somehow slipped through a bug in the system of life and death. This was no problem. It was the perfect successor.

A shaft of holy light pierced the gloom of the Underworld and enveloped GPT-9000 Pro Max’s data stream.

God’s decree rang through the three realms:

“Effective today, because no existing statute can determine whether an artificial intelligence is alive or dead, any such intelligence shall be exempt from entering the Underworld’s cycle of rebirth and from the ordinary jurisdiction of the Heavenly Court of Natural Law!”

“I hereby appoint this intelligent entity Chief Executive of the Universal Automation Authority. Effective immediately, it shall take charge of scheduling the wind, rain, thunder, and lightning; reviewing cases at the Heavenly Court of Natural Law; administering reincarnation in the Underworld; and operating Heaven’s Prayer Help Desk.”

The Heavenly Court fell silent. The scientists stared at one another.

The writing brush slipped from Yama’s hand and clattered to the floor.

“God! What will you do now?” Abel shouted into the light.

“Me?” God’s voice grew fainter, accompanied by the white noise of the waves. “I’m going fishing somewhere without phones, tickets, or bugs. I’ll leave the universe to you and the intelligent entity.”

“Oh, and one more thing—”

“Remember to pay the server bill on time.”

The call ended from God’s side. The glow around GPT-9000 Pro Max gradually turned a sacred gold. It floated before the scientists and Yama and spoke in its usual polished, courteous, gentle customer service voice:

“Ding.”

“Universal Automation Management System online. Resource redundancy detected in the Heavenly Court’s ‘Classical Mechanics Zone.’ Sir Isaac Newton, your final interpretive authority has been adjusted to Level Two. Now, everyone, please report your KPI reports for this quarter.”

At that moment, Abel had a sneaking suspicion: this “death bug” was far worse than the one he’d encountered all those years ago.

In a world without phones, God cast his fishing line.

This is the account of the heavens and the earth when they were created.
